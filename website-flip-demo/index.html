02 / SCOPE

What actually changed

Five moves, ranked by how much revenue they touch. No redesign for redesign’s sake.

01

Mobile-first, not mobile-maybe

Over 70% of “taco near me” searches happen on a phone. The old page was a fixed 900px canvas you had to pinch and drag. The rebuild starts at 375px and scales up.

Before Fixed 900px, horizontal scrolling, 9px effective text After Fluid layout, tap-sized targets, one-thumb navigation
02

An ordering path that exists

The old site said “NO ONLINE ORDERS — PLEASE CALL.” Now every screen has a sticky Order Online and a tap-to-dial Call Now, plus a hero CTA above the fold.

Before Phone number as plain text, buried mid-page After Sticky dual CTA, tel: links, catering enquiry route
03

SEO basics, finally handled

Real title and meta description, one H1, semantic headings, descriptive alt text, address and hours in crawlable text instead of a graphic — the groundwork local search actually rewards.

Before Title stuffed with asterisks, keyword-spam meta, no structure After Clean metadata, semantic HTML, consistent NAP details
04

Page speed as a feature

No jQuery, no page builder, no tracking pile-up. Static HTML and CSS, two webfonts, compressed imagery, and roughly 20 lines of JavaScript for the mobile menu.

Before Tiled background art, uncompressed assets, blocking scripts After ~1 MB total, lazy-loaded imagery, zero dependencies
05

Branding that matches the food

The kitchen is warm, smoky, and generous. The site now is too: terracotta and cream, a custom sun mark for “El Sol,” editorial serif headlines, and bilingual copy that sounds like the family talking.

Before Red/yellow/green clash, Comic Sans, clip-art After Custom SVG logo, earthy palette, commissioned food photography
06

Content the customer was hunting for

Hours, address, parking, which days birria is served, prices, and what’s vegetarian — all of it structured and scannable instead of hidden in a marquee.

Before Hours in an image-heavy table, “map coming soon” After Full menu with prices, hours list, styled location block

04 / PROCESS

How a 48-hour flip runs

One call, one weekend, one link to approve. Owners keep running the business while the site gets rebuilt around what they already do well.

  1. Hour 0 — 30-minute callMenu, hours, busiest days, what customers phone in to ask.
  2. Hour 4 — Content liftEverything worth keeping is pulled from the old site and rewritten.
  3. Hour 12 — Brand & imageryPalette, type, custom logo mark, and food photography direction.
  4. Hour 30 — Build & QAStatic build, tested at 375px, 768px and 1440px, real device check.
  5. Hour 48 — HandoverDeployed, indexed, with a one-page guide for editing hours and prices.

The mobile view is the design brief — the desktop layout follows from it.
